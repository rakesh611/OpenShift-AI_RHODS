# 20. Audit process
> Corporate L3 implementation handbook for Red Hat OpenShift AI enterprise environments.
## Document metadata
- **Domain:** Enterprise Governance
- **Topic:** Audit process
- **Primary focus:** repeatable planning, evidence collection, sampling, testing, findings, remediation, and closure for platform and AI controls.
- **Audience:** Senior OpenShift administrators, OpenShift AI administrators, MLOps engineers, SREs, security engineers, compliance teams, architects, and technical governance leads.

## Executive summary
Audit process governs repeatable planning, evidence collection, sampling, testing, findings, remediation, and closure for platform and AI controls. In an enterprise OpenShift AI platform, the control must work across the dashboard, Kubernetes API, Git repositories, container registries, object storage, model registries, pipeline systems, monitoring systems, ticketing systems, and human approval workflows.
A mature implementation does not depend on a wiki page alone. It combines a written standard, machine-enforced policy, documented ownership, approved exception handling, observability, evidence retention, and periodic effectiveness testing.
The design in this guide uses four control layers:
1. **Intent layer:** charter, policy, standard, RACI, risk appetite, and exception criteria.
2. **Preventive layer:** RBAC, admission policy, signed artifacts, Git protections, namespace controls, network isolation, and immutable identifiers.
3. **Detective layer:** inventory scans, drift detection, metrics, alerts, periodic access reviews, audit logs, and evidence reconciliation.
4. **Corrective layer:** remediation runbooks, rollback, quarantine, revocation, retirement, incident response, and root-cause follow-up.

### RACI design rules
1. Do not assign the same person as requester and final approver for a high-risk production decision.
2. Use team or role identifiers in policy, but retain named accountability in the approval system.
3. Define deputies for leave, incident response, and disaster-recovery operation.
4. Ensure every artifact has exactly one accountable owner even when multiple teams contribute.
5. Map service accounts to a human-owned service and escalation path.
6. Review RACI membership after organizational restructuring, role changes, and major platform upgrades.
7. Record conflicts of interest and require independent approval where applicable.
8. Define who can declare an emergency exception and who must review it afterward.
9. Define the maximum decision time for each approval stage.
10. Retain superseded RACI versions to prove historical accountability.

### Branch protection
1. Require pull requests for the default branch.
2. Require successful policy, schema, YAML, security, and conformance checks.
3. Require at least two reviewers for production policy changes.
4. Require CODEOWNERS review from platform and governance teams.
5. Disallow force push and branch deletion.
6. Require signed commits or verified identities where organizational tooling supports it.
7. Restrict pipeline secrets to protected environments.
8. Retain build logs and validation reports according to evidence policy.
9. Tag released policy bundles and record the deployed digest.
10. Use a break-glass process for emergency changes and perform retrospective review.

- [ ] Does the change preserve the stated control objective for Audit process?

- [ ] Is backward compatibility documented?

### Control 01: Audit process — Audit Universe
**Control objective:** Ensure that repeatable planning, evidence collection, sampling, testing, findings, remediation, and closure for platform and AI controls is governed through the `audit universe` and that the risk of incomplete audit logs remains within approved tolerance.
**Control type:** preventive.
**Frequency:** per change.
**Accountable owner:** AI platform owner.
**Responsible operator:** MLOps platform engineer.
**Implementation:**
1. Define the required data fields, allowed values, approval rules, and retention for the audit universe.
2. Store the desired state in a protected Git repository and identify the deployed commit or artifact digest.
3. Enforce mandatory fields in CI and at admission where technically possible.
4. Apply least-privilege RBAC so only authorized roles can create, approve, or modify the governed record.
5. Generate recurring inventory and drift reports across all in-scope clusters and environments.
6. Route noncompliance to a named remediation queue with severity, owner, and due date.
7. Require a time-bounded exception when immediate remediation is not possible.
**Verification:**
1. Select a representative sandbox resource and confirm its audit universe matches the authoritative source.
2. Execute a negative test that omits or corrupts one mandatory field and confirm the control blocks or detects it.
3. Confirm the audit trail records who performed the action, what changed, when it changed, and the result.
4. Reconcile the operational resource, Git desired state, approval record, and evidence output.
**Required evidence:**
- approved request record.
- Timestamped validation output for control 01.
- Traceability from the audit universe to the related change, owner, environment, and exception record.
**Failure response:**
1. Contain exposure related to incomplete audit logs.
2. Preserve logs and configuration before making corrective changes.
3. Restore the last approved state through GitOps or an approved emergency procedure.
4. Record root cause, control gap, affected resources, and preventive action.
**Maturity progression:**
- Level 1: documented manually and applied inconsistently.
- Level 2: repeatable procedure with named ownership.
- Level 3: automated validation and centralized evidence.
- Level 4: risk-based adaptive enforcement and measured effectiveness.
- Level 5: continuous control monitoring with trend-based improvement.

5. An exception is active, expired, malformed, or not recognized.
6. Evidence collector scope, credentials, or clock synchronization is incorrect.

2. Resource metadata differs from the authoritative catalog.
3. Admission or CI policy is running an outdated bundle.
4. The acting user or service account has unexpected privilege.
5. An exception is active, expired, malformed, or not recognized.
6. Evidence collector scope, credentials, or clock synchronization is incorrect.
**Diagnostic procedure:**
1. Record cluster, namespace, resource kind, resource name, timestamp, user, and request ID.
2. Export the resource YAML and relevant events without editing the live object.
3. Compare observed labels, annotations, spec, status, and managed fields with Git desired state.
4. Check the GitOps application health and last synchronized revision.
5. Check admission-policy version, decision logs, and failure mode.
6. Run `oc auth can-i` for the exact subject and operation.
7. Validate external catalogs and approval references.
8. Confirm the exception ID, approver, compensating control, and expiry.
9. Re-run the evidence query for a narrowly scoped sample.
10. Determine whether the issue is a control failure, telemetry failure, or display/reporting failure.
**Safe remediation:**
1. Contain production risk before correcting metadata or policy.
2. Use a pull request or approved emergency change; do not patch around the policy without a record.
3. Synchronize the corrected desired state and verify admission behavior.
4. Re-run conformance tests and evidence reconciliation.
5. Close or update the exception and record root cause.
**Escalate when:** production safety, regulated data, external exposure, repeated bypass, evidence integrity, or privilege escalation may be affected.

### Runbook: Restore compliance for Audit process

**Trigger conditions:**
- A production control violation is detected.
- A policy decision unexpectedly allows or denies a resource.
- Governance evidence is missing or contradictory.
- A time-bounded exception has expired.
- An audit or security review identifies a material gap.

**Pre-checks:**
1. Confirm incident or change record exists.
2. Confirm the acting engineer has approved access.
3. Identify affected clusters, namespaces, resources, owners, consumers, and data classifications.
4. Preserve current YAML, events, logs, policy decision, Git revision, and approval reference.
5. Confirm whether customer impact or model risk requires incident escalation.

**Execution:**
1. Quarantine or restrict the affected resource when continued operation creates unacceptable risk.
2. Notify the accountable owner and on-call platform team.
3. Identify the last known approved desired state.
4. Validate that the desired state itself remains approved and safe.
5. Create a corrective pull request or invoke the approved emergency procedure.
6. Run schema, policy, security, and conformance tests.
7. Obtain required independent approval.
8. Synchronize the target environment through GitOps.
9. Verify resource health and control decision.
10. Re-run population-level compliance scan.
11. Verify evidence generation and retention.
12. Remove temporary containment only after control restoration.
13. Update the incident/change record with exact timestamps and results.
14. Create follow-up actions for root cause and systemic prevention.
15. Close the exception or issue only after independent verification.
**Rollback criteria:**
- Roll back or contain immediately if remediation increases the risk of incomplete audit logs.
- Roll back or contain immediately if remediation increases the risk of evidence altered after collection.

### Topic closeout: Audit process
A successful audit process implementation is not measured only by document publication. It is successful when ownership is clear, controls are enforced proportionately, exceptions expire, evidence is reproducible, operators can recover safely, and governance decisions improve over time.
