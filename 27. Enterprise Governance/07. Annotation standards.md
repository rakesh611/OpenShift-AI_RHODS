## Executive summary
Annotation standards governs non-selectable metadata for descriptions, approvals, references, expiry, documentation, and audit context. In an enterprise OpenShift AI platform, the control must work across the dashboard, Kubernetes API, Git repositories, container registries, object storage, model registries, pipeline systems, monitoring systems, ticketing systems, and human approval workflows.
A mature implementation does not depend on a wiki page alone. It combines a written standard, machine-enforced policy, documented ownership, approved exception handling, observability, evidence retention, and periodic effectiveness testing.
The design in this guide uses four control layers:
1. **Intent layer:** charter, policy, standard, RACI, risk appetite, and exception criteria.
2. **Preventive layer:** RBAC, admission policy, signed artifacts, Git protections, namespace controls, network isolation, and immutable identifiers.
3. **Detective layer:** inventory scans, drift detection, metrics, alerts, periodic access reviews, audit logs, and evidence reconciliation.
4. **Corrective layer:** remediation runbooks, rollback, quarantine, revocation, retirement, incident response, and root-cause follow-up.

### RACI design rules
1. Do not assign the same person as requester and final approver for a high-risk production decision.
2. Use team or role identifiers in policy, but retain named accountability in the approval system.
3. Define deputies for leave, incident response, and disaster-recovery operation.
4. Ensure every artifact has exactly one accountable owner even when multiple teams contribute.
5. Map service accounts to a human-owned service and escalation path.
6. Review RACI membership after organizational restructuring, role changes, and major platform upgrades.
7. Record conflicts of interest and require independent approval where applicable.
8. Define who can declare an emergency exception and who must review it afterward.
9. Define the maximum decision time for each approval stage.
10. Retain superseded RACI versions to prove historical accountability.

### Branch protection
1. Require pull requests for the default branch.
2. Require successful policy, schema, YAML, security, and conformance checks.
3. Require at least two reviewers for production policy changes.
4. Require CODEOWNERS review from platform and governance teams.
5. Disallow force push and branch deletion.
6. Require signed commits or verified identities where organizational tooling supports it.
7. Restrict pipeline secrets to protected environments.
8. Retain build logs and validation reports according to evidence policy.
9. Tag released policy bundles and record the deployed digest.
10. Use a break-glass process for emergency changes and perform retrospective review.
